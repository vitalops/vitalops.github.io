01 · Today

How this work gets done in most companies

  1. Documents arrive by email as PDFs, scans and spreadsheets, often several for one order.

  2. Someone opens each file, finds the order or shipment it belongs to, and types the figures into the ERP.

  3. They compare the invoice with the purchase order and the delivery note, line by line.

  4. When something doesn't match, they email the supplier or forwarder and wait for a reply.

  5. At month end, finance matches what was booked against bank and carrier statements.

The work grows with volume. The usual fix is to hire, and new staff take months to learn which supplier sends what.

02 · Why it's still manual

Most teams have tried to automate this before

The attempts tend to fail for the same four reasons.

The ERP has no usable API

Core systems are often 10 to 20 years old and heavily customised. Some can only be reached through their screens, a nightly file export, or a database nobody wants to touch.

Templates break

Template-based OCR and RPA need a rule for each document layout. A company with 300 suppliers has hundreds of layouts, and suppliers change them without notice.

Most of the time goes on exceptions

Mismatched quantities, missing certificates and partial shipments take far longer than clean documents. A tool that stops at the first exception saves little.

Nobody maintains it

Software vendors hand over a licence and IT has a backlog. When a portal changes its login page, the bot stops and staff go back to doing it by hand.

04 · How it works

Where the effort goes on a real project

Reading a clean PDF takes little effort. Most of a project goes into the five areas below.

Connecting to your systems

ERP · email · portals · EDI · SFTP

We connect through whatever each system allows, in this order of preference: an API, a read-only database view, scheduled file exports, EDI, email. If a system has none of these, we operate its screens through a browser or remote desktop session, as a person would.

Each connection has a health check that runs every few minutes, and we get an alert when one fails.

Reading the documents

scans · photos · handwriting · tables

We fine-tune document models on a sample of your own files. They handle new layouts without a template, as well as stamps over text, handwritten notes, tables that run across pages, and documents in more than one language.

Before we quote, we run your sample through and give you the accuracy for each field you care about.

Handling exceptions

review queue · corrections

When the system is unsure, or a check fails, the item goes to a review screen. It shows the documents, the order it was matched to, and the reason it was flagged. Your staff correct it on that screen.

We retrain on those corrections, so fewer items need review as the months go by.

Keeping it running

monitoring · fixes · on-call

Suppliers change invoice layouts, portals change their screens, and your rules change at quarter end. We find out from the health checks or from a rise in review items, and we fix it. This is covered by the monthly fee.

Keeping a record

audit trail · exports

Each figure booked in your ERP links back to the document it came from, the checks it passed, and the person who approved it, if anyone did. Auditors can follow any entry back to its source.

06 · Project plan

A typical first project takes about six weeks

We start with one process. Your team keeps doing the work as usual until the results have been checked against theirs.

  1. Week 0

    Process review

    A call to walk through the process. You send 50 to 200 sample documents, including the difficult ones.

  2. Weeks 1–2

    Connect and measure

    We get read access to the systems involved, tune the document models on your samples, and report accuracy per field.

  3. Weeks 3–4

    Build and test

    We build the checks and the review screen, and test them on past cases where the right answer is known.

  4. Weeks 5–6

    Run in parallel

    The system processes live items while your team works as before. A person approves every output, and we compare the two.

  5. Week 7 on

    Go live

    Only flagged items need a person. You get a weekly report, and we start on the next process if you want one.

07 · Data and security

Your documents stay where you decide

  • Models we host ourselves. We run open-weight models on servers we operate. Your documents are not sent to OpenAI, Google or any other model provider.
  • Three places it can run. Our servers, with storage kept separate for each client. Your own cloud account. Or your data centre, with no internet connection if required.
  • Your own access policies apply. Staff sign in through your single sign-on. Our engineers work under your access policies, and you decide how long data is kept.
